        Why breath, not blood

## Gas your body can't make on its own

        Human cells don't produce hydrogen. Bacteria do — and only when they get hold of sugar your small intestine failed to absorb. That gas has nowhere to go but into your bloodstream, up through your lungs, and out.

        01
#### You drink a measured sugar

Lactose, fructose, or glucose — chosen based on what we're screening for.

        02
#### Undigested sugar reaches your gut bacteria

If your body can't break it down upstream, it passes through intact.

        03
#### Bacteria ferment it, releasing hydrogen

This is the same fermentation that happens in bread dough or a compost pile.

        04
#### The gas surfaces in your breath

We measure it every 15–20 minutes to trace the exact rise.

        What it detects

## Three conditions, one breath

The same protocol, run with different sugars, can point to three separate causes behind bloating, cramping, and irregular digestion.

### Lactose Intolerance

          Tests whether your body produces enough lactase to break down the sugar in milk and dairy.

          Substrate: Lactose

### Fructose Malabsorption

          Checks how well your small intestine absorbs fruit sugar, often behind reactions to apples, honey, and soft drinks.

          Substrate: Fructose

### SIBO

Screens for small intestinal bacterial overgrowth — bacteria colonising a part of the gut where they shouldn't thrive.

          Substrate: Glucose

## Getting ready matters as much as the test itself

Certain foods and medicines feed the same bacteria we're trying to measure, which can throw off your reading. Follow this the day before your appointment.

#### Do this

            Fast for 8–12 hours before your slot — water is fine

            - Eat a plain dinner the night before: rice, chicken, or fish

            - Arrive without perfume or strong scents near the mouth

            - Bring your doctor's referral, if you have one

#### Avoid beforehand

            - Antibiotics for 4 weeks prior, unless your doctor says otherwise

            - High-fibre foods, beans, and whole grains the day before

            - Smoking on the morning of the test

            - Strenuous exercise right before your appointment

## Before you book

          Does the test hurt? +

No. It's entirely breath-based — you drink a sweet solution and breathe into a bag at intervals. No needles, no fasting beyond the night before.

          How long am I actually on-site? +

Most protocols run 2–3 hours, since we need to sample your breath every 15–20 minutes to see the full curve, not just one point.

          Can children take this test? +

Yes, it's commonly used for children too, with a dose adjusted to their weight. A parent stays on-site for the full session.

          Will my report tell me what to eat? +

The report gives your doctor a clear diagnosis — dietary guidance is something to work through with them afterward, based on your result.

          Do I need a doctor's referral? +

It helps but isn't required. You can book directly and share the report with your doctor afterward, or bring a referral if you already have one.
